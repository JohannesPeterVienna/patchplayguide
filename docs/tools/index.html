Tools

Tools for graphics settings and testing

Six tools and two references. The calculators work only with documented factors and arithmetic; the finder gives rule-of-thumb guidance and the translator an editorial estimate; the test log works only with numbers you measure yourself. All of them run in your browser, can be used with the keyboard, and let you copy, link or print the result.

Upscaler Resolution Calculator

What DLSS, FSR and XeSS really render on your display in each mode – internal resolution, megapixels and pixel share, from the vendors’ documented factors.

Calculate →

Frame Time Calculator

Frames per second in milliseconds: does a frame rate divide your refresh rate, fit your VRR window, and what does frame generation show versus render?

Calculate →

Settings Test Log

Type in your own test runs before and after a change. The log shows medians and spread, and whether the runs separate or the result is undecided – with a printable worksheet.

Open the log →

Bottleneck Finder

Low FPS, stutter, tearing or input lag? A few questions lead to the likely cause – GPU, CPU, VRAM, shaders, a limiter or the display chain – and the next steps.

Start the finder →

Settings Translator

Twenty-five common graphics options with our editorial estimate of their GPU, CPU and VRAM load. Pick your bottleneck to see what to test first.

Open translator →

Patch Decoder

Paste patch notes and sort every line into + improvement, ~ change or − removal, by category and platform, with a checklist to tick off.

Open decoder →

Setting-name map

The same setting under different names: every DLSS, FSR and XeSS preset, frame generation, latency features, VRR and console terms, each with its official source.

Search the map →

Console upgrades by game

What PS5 Pro, QSSR and Nintendo Switch 2 upgrades change in each game, as the developers and platform holders state it – with date and source per entry.

Search the list →
