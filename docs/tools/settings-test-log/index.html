Settings Test Log: is the difference real, or just noise?

Published by AdStern, Vienna

Reviewed November 18, 20265 min readHow we check pages

You changed one setting and the counter shows three more frames per second. Is that the setting, or would the next run have shown the same without it? Type in the results of a few runs before and after the change. The log calculates the median and the run-to-run spread of each setup and tells you whether the difference stands clear of that spread. There is also a blank worksheet to print and fill in by hand.

What the log calculates

For each setup the log takes your runs and shows three things. The median is the middle run when they are sorted; unlike the mean, one odd run – a background download, a loading hitch – does not drag it. The lowest–highest range shows how far your runs scatter when nothing changes; this is your noise. The spread is that range in fps and as a percentage of the median.

Then it compares the two setups: the difference of the medians in fps and percent, and the same difference in milliseconds per frame, because that is the unit you feel (see the Frame Time Calculator). Finally it gives a verdict with one deliberately strict rule: the difference is called clear only if every run of one setup is faster than every run of the other. If the ranges overlap, the log says so and does not declare a winner.

Why the rule is “no overlap”

The rule needs no assumptions about your hardware and no statistics software, and its error rate can be counted. Suppose a setting changes nothing at all. Then your six runs are six draws from the same pot, and every ordering of them is equally likely. With three runs per setup there are 20 ways to split six runs into two groups of three, and only two of them place all runs of one group above all runs of the other. A clean split therefore appears by luck in 1 of 10 tests. More runs make the rule much stricter:

How often a clean split appears by pure chance
Runs per setupPossible orderings of all runsChance that one setup wins every run by luck

Counting argument, not a measurement: if a setting changes nothing, every ordering of the runs is equally likely, and only two of them put all runs of one setup above all runs of the other.

This is why the testing guide recommends at least three runs and suggests five when a decision matters: with five runs per setup, a clean split that is pure luck happens once in 126 tests. The price of the strict rule is that small real differences can hide in the overlap. That is acceptable for the purpose – a difference you cannot separate from your own noise is also one you will not notice while playing.

The test protocol in six steps

  1. Fix the scene. A built-in benchmark, or a route of 30 to 60 seconds you can repeat exactly. Write it into the first field.
  2. Warm up. Play the route once without recording, so shaders and assets are loaded. After a game or driver update this run matters most – see shader compilation stutter.
  3. Record setup A at least three times. Note the average FPS and, if your overlay shows it, the 1% low of each run.
  4. Change exactly one setting and restart the game if it asks for it.
  5. Record setup B the same number of times, on the same route.
  6. Read the verdict, and add what you saw: a number does not show shimmer, blur or pop-in.

The full reasoning behind each step – why one change at a time, how to pick a scene, what to measure with – is in how to test a settings change properly. For what the numbers mean, read FPS, frame time and 1% lows.

Limits

The log knows nothing but the numbers you type. It cannot check whether the runs were comparable, whether a cap or V-Sync flattened them (identical values in every run are a hint), or whether an update changed the scene between A and B. It is built for one question – did this one change make a difference here – not for comparing hardware or publishing benchmarks. Your entries are stored only in this browser. “Copy link with my numbers” puts the values into the link’s address so that you can send it to someone or keep it; nothing is stored on a server.

Frequently asked questions

How many test runs do I need to compare two settings?

At least three per setup. With three runs each, a clean split still appears by chance in 1 of 10 tests where nothing differs; with five runs each, in 1 of 126.

Why does the log use the median and not the average of my runs?

The median is the middle run and ignores a single outlier, such as a run disturbed by a background task. With three runs, one bad run shifts the mean but not the median.

My runs overlap – does that mean the setting does nothing?

It means your test cannot separate the effect from the normal run-to-run spread. Add runs or use a longer, more repeatable route. If the ranges still overlap, the two setups perform the same in that scene for practical purposes.

Does the tool measure my frame rate?

No. It runs in the browser and cannot see your game. You measure with an overlay or capture tool and type the results in.
