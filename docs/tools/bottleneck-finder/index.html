Bottleneck Finder: from symptom to cause in a minute

Published by AdStern, Vienna

Reviewed November 18, 20268 min readHow we check pages

Low frame rates, stutter and laggy controls feel similar but have different causes – and the wrong fix wastes time or image quality. Answer one question at a time about what you see; the finder leads to the most likely cause, the next steps and the guide that explains it in depth.

Bottleneck Finder

One question at a time · about 1 minute

    How the finder decides

    The finder is a decision tree with thirteen questions, twenty-two possible results and 30 routes between them. It starts with the symptom you notice most – low frame rate, stutter, tearing, delayed controls, a problem after an update or a missing console option – then asks for one simple observation at a time – usually something you can check in under a minute, such as whether the frame rate rises when you lower the resolution. You can go back one step or start over at any time; the trail above the question shows your answers so far.

    Every result names the likely cause, explains it in one sentence and lists what to do now, in order. When a result points to a GPU, CPU or VRAM limit, the finder also preselects that bottleneck in the Settings Translator, so the lower-first list is ready when you open it. Nothing is sent anywhere; only that one preselection is stored in your browser.

    Symptoms and their usual causes

    The table summarizes the paths through the tree. It is useful on its own if you already know the symptom well.

    SymptomMost likely causeQuick test
    Low FPS everywhere, GPU at 95–100%GPU-boundLower render resolution a lot: FPS rises clearly
    Low FPS, GPU clearly below full useCPU-bound or a frame limiterLower resolution: FPS barely changes; check caps and V-Sync

    Two symptoms are often confused. Low FPS means the average frame rate is too low, and the game feels sluggish all the time. Stutter means individual frames take much longer than the rest – the average can look fine while motion jerks. Low FPS is about throughput, stutter about consistency; they have different causes, and the finder separates them in its very first question. If you want to see the difference in numbers, read FPS, frame time and 1% lows explained.

    The one test you should know

    On PC and handheld PCs, the finder relies on this resolution test because it needs no special tools. Use a demanding but repeatable spot, look at the frame-rate counter, then lower the render resolution a lot – for example from native to the upscaler’s Performance mode, or the render scale to 50%. Stand still or repeat the same short movement, because different views have different costs.

    If you have a performance overlay, GPU usage confirms the answer. A GPU near full load while the frame rate is low points to the graphics card. A GPU clearly below full load – the finder uses “under about 85%” as a rough line – points to the CPU, a frame cap or V-Sync. The threshold is a rule of thumb, not a precise value: some games never show 100% even when GPU-bound, and short spikes are normal.

    What the finder cannot see

    The finder knows only your answers. It cannot read your hardware, so a few situations need extra care. Laptops and handhelds throttle when hot or on battery, which looks like a GPU limit with lower clocks. Background software – downloads, recording, browser tabs with video – can cause stutter that looks like a game problem. And some problems are simply bugs in a specific update; the Patch Decoder helps you find “known issue” lines in the patch notes you paste.

    On consoles, the finder points you to mode choices and display settings rather than single graphics options, because that is what PS5, Xbox and Switch 2 games expose. The console guides – PS5 modes, Xbox Series S vs X and VRR and 120 Hz – explain what each mode trades.

    Every route at a glance

    The complete tree, generated from the same data the tool uses. Follow the indentation: each line is an answer, the arrow leads to a result, and a question in between narrows it down. If you already know your symptom well, you can read your result here without clicking.

    What do you notice most?

    • The frame rate is low most of the time

      Are you on PC or a handheld PC, where you can change resolution or the upscaler?

      • Yes – PC or handheld PC

        Lower the render resolution a lot (or set the upscaler to Performance) and look at the same spot. Does the frame rate rise clearly?

        • Yes, clearly (roughly a quarter or more) → Likely GPU-bound
        • It barely changes

          Open a performance overlay. While FPS is low, is GPU usage well below full load – for example under about 85%?

          • Yes, the GPU is clearly not fully used

            Is a frame cap, V-Sync, a battery-saver mode or a laptop power plan active?

            • Yes, or I am not sure → A limiter caps the frame rate
            • No, nothing limits the frame rate → Likely CPU-bound
          • No, the GPU is near 95–100% → GPU at full load – power, heat or simply too demanding
          • I cannot see GPU usage → Measure first
        • I am not sure

          Open a performance overlay. While FPS is low, is GPU usage well below full load – for example under about 85%?

          • Yes, the GPU is clearly not fully used

            Is a frame cap, V-Sync, a battery-saver mode or a laptop power plan active?

            • Yes, or I am not sure → A limiter caps the frame rate
            • No, nothing limits the frame rate → Likely CPU-bound
          • No, the GPU is near 95–100% → GPU at full load – power, heat or simply too demanding
          • I cannot see GPU usage → Measure first
      • No – PS5, Xbox or Switch 2 → Console: choose the right mode, not single settings
    • Stutter or hitches – the average is fine, but it jerks

      When does the stutter happen?

      • The first time I see new areas or effects – worse after a game or driver update → Shader compilation stutter
      • When moving fast or entering new areas, even on repeat visits

        Is video memory (VRAM) usage close to your graphics card’s total, or did it start after raising textures?

        • Yes → Video memory (VRAM) is full
        • No → Asset streaming hitches
      • At random or at a steady rhythm, wherever I am → Frame pacing or background interruptions
    • FPS drops only in busy places: cities, crowds, big fights

      In one of those busy spots, does lowering the render resolution raise the frame rate clearly?

      • No, hardly at all → CPU limit in dense scenes
      • Yes, clearly → Heavy GPU effects in busy scenes
      • I am on a console → Console: choose the right mode, not single settings
    • Blurry or late-loading textures, or trouble after raising texture quality → Video memory (VRAM) is full
    • Controls feel delayed although the frame rate looks fine

      Is frame generation switched on?

      • Yes → Frame generation adds latency
      • No → Render queue or V-Sync latency
    • It started right after a game update

      What changed after the update?

      • Stutter in the first minutes or in new areas → Shader compilation stutter
      • The game looks different, or my settings seem reset → Settings or defaults changed
      • The frame rate is lower everywhere → Possible performance regression
    • Screen tearing – the image splits into horizontal bands

      Does your display support variable refresh rate (G-SYNC, FreeSync or HDMI VRR), and is it switched on in the display and in the system settings?

      • Yes, VRR is on

        When it tears, is the frame rate above the display’s maximum refresh rate?

        • Yes – or the frame rate is uncapped → Frame rate above the VRR window
        • No, it tears at low frame rates → Frame rate below the VRR window
      • No, or I am not sure → Tearing without VRR: sync or cap to an even frame rate
    • Console: a 120 Hz, 40 fps or VRR option is missing, or a mode judders

      What exactly happens on the console?

      • The game offers no 120 Hz or 40 fps mode, or the VRR switch is greyed out → The 120 Hz/VRR chain is broken somewhere
      • A 40 fps or “balanced” mode looks uneven → A 40 fps mode needs 120 Hz output
      • The frame rate is simply too low or unsteady → Console: choose the right mode, not single settings
    • Handheld: battery drains fast or the frame rate swings → Handheld power budget

    After you have a result

    Change one thing at a time and test the same scene again. If the result was “GPU-bound”, start with the upscaler mode; if it was “CPU-bound”, start with view distance or crowds. A change that does not help should be reverted before you try the next one, otherwise you lose image quality without knowing why. The testing guide explains a routine that takes about ten minutes, and the Settings Test Log tells you whether the difference between your runs is larger than their normal spread.

    Every result can be kept: “Copy link to this result” gives an address that reopens exactly this result, “Copy result as text” puts the cause, your answers and the steps on the clipboard, and “Print” prints the page with the result.

    Frequently asked questions

    Do I need a performance overlay?

    Not for most paths. The resolution test works with any frame-rate counter. GPU usage from an overlay helps to separate a CPU limit from a frame cap, and the finder tells you when you need it.

    Why does lowering resolution not help my FPS?

    Then the graphics card was not the limit. The CPU, a frame cap, V-Sync or a power limit holds the frame rate down; the finder asks which one in the next steps.

    Is stutter after an update a hardware problem?

    Usually not. Game and driver updates often invalidate the shader cache, so the game compiles shaders again on first use. It typically fades as you play through the same areas.

    Does the finder work for consoles?

    Yes, but it leads to mode and display choices, because consoles do not expose single graphics options in most games.

    What is stored when I use the finder?

    Only the bottleneck it found, in your browser’s local storage, so the Settings Translator can preselect it. Your answers appear as a short code in the page address so that a result can be reopened or shared; they are not saved on a server.
