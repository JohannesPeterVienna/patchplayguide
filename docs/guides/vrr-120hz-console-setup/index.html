VRR and 120 Hz on consoles: setup guide

Published by AdStern, Vienna

Reviewed November 3, 20264 min readHow we check pages

A 120 Hz signal and variable refresh rate (VRR) can make console games feel noticeably smoother – but only if the TV, the HDMI port, the cable, the console and the game all play along. One wrong link in the chain and the option is greyed out or silently off. This guide goes through the chain in order and explains what VRR can and cannot fix.

What 120 Hz and VRR do

120 Hz output lets the display refresh twice as often as a 60 Hz signal. Games can then run at up to 120 fps, or use 40 fps modes that fit evenly into 120 Hz (three refreshes per frame).

Variable refresh rate lets the display wait for each new frame instead of refreshing on a fixed clock. Without it, a game running at, say, 52 fps on a 60 Hz display either tears (frames switch mid-refresh) or judders (some frames are shown twice). With VRR, the display simply refreshes at 52 Hz. VRR does not make the game faster; it makes uneven frame rates look smooth.

The TV setting that unlocks full HDMI bandwidth has different names by brand – look for “enhanced”, “input signal plus”, “HDMI ultra HD deep colour” or similar in the TV’s manual. Without it, the console may only see a 60 Hz display.

Console settings

PS5: Settings → Screen and Video → Video Output. Set “Enable 120 Hz Output” to Automatic and turn VRR on; the menu also offers applying VRR to games without official support, which helps some and causes flicker or other issues in others. The PS5 shows VRR options only when the connected display reports support.

Xbox Series X|S: Settings → General → TV & display options. Choose 120 Hz as the refresh rate and, under Video modes, allow variable refresh rate and Auto Low-Latency Mode. The 4K TV details screen shows what the console detected.

Switch 2: According to Nintendo’s specifications, docked TV output reaches 120 Hz at lower resolutions in supported games. VRR was available on the built-in screen from launch; since the system update of September 10, 2026 it also works on the TV in docked mode, with a compatible VRR TV and in games that support it. See Switch 2 docked vs handheld.

Menu names change with system updates; if an option has moved, the platform’s support site has the current path.

VRR ranges and what VRR cannot fix

Every VRR display has a range – for TVs the lower limit is commonly somewhere around 40 to 48 Hz, the upper limit the panel’s maximum refresh rate. Within the range, VRR works as described. Below it, the display falls back to fixed refresh behavior, unless the system uses low framerate compensation (showing each frame twice to stay in range), which depends on the display and console.

That has two consequences. A 30 fps mode sits below most TV ranges, so VRR does little for it. And a performance mode that drops from 60 into the high 40s benefits a lot. Some games offer an “unlocked” frame rate with VRR – for example running between 60 and 120 fps – which is only smooth with VRR enabled.

VRR also cannot fix stutter: a single frame that takes 100 ms will still be visible, VRR or not. For the difference between low frame rates and stutter, see FPS, frame time and 1% lows.

Troubleshooting

  • 120 Hz option missing or greyed out: check the TV port and its enhanced signal setting first, then the cable.
  • Black screen or flickering after enabling 120 Hz: try the cable that came with the console and a direct connection without receiver.
  • VRR enabled but no difference: the game may run at a locked frame rate, or below the TV’s VRR range.
  • Brightness flicker in dark scenes with VRR: a known behavior on some panels at low frame rates; it can be a reason to turn VRR off for that game.
  • After a TV firmware update, re-check the enhanced signal settings – updates can reset them.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need HDMI 2.1 for 120 Hz on PS5 and Xbox?

For 4K at 120 Hz, yes – an HDMI 2.1-capable port and a suitable cable. Some displays support 120 Hz at lower resolutions over older HDMI versions.

Does VRR increase input lag?

No. VRR usually reduces the delay compared with V-Sync, because the display does not make frames wait for a fixed refresh.

Why does my 30 fps mode not benefit from VRR?

30 fps is below the VRR range of most TVs. VRR helps most with frame rates that fluctuate inside the range, for example between 48 and 60.

Which HDMI cable do I need?

The one supplied with the console, or a certified Ultra High Speed HDMI cable. Older cables may limit bandwidth.
