How to read patch notes: what each kind of change means for your settings

By the Patch Play Guide editorial team, AdStern

Reviewed November 2, 20264 min readHow we check pages

Patch notes mix balance changes, bug fixes, new content and technical changes in one list, often hundreds of lines long. Only a handful usually matter for how the game runs and looks on your screen. This guide shows how to find them, how to read the typical wording, and which action each kind of line calls for.

Where to find the real patch notes

Use the official source: the developer’s or publisher’s website, the game’s news page on Steam, the platform holder’s news channels, or the update history in the game itself. Console and PC versions often get separate notes, and hotfixes are sometimes only announced on social media or in community forums run by the developer. Summaries on other sites – including ours – are no substitute for the original wording when you decide what to do.

Two things are easy to miss: the platform labels (a line may apply only to PS5 or only to PC) and the known issues section, often at the very end. Both decide whether a change concerns you.

Reading the wording

Patch notes are written by many people under time pressure, and some words are vaguer than they look:

  • “Improved performance” without a place or platform often refers to specific scenes or hardware. Test where you play.
  • “Optimized” can mean faster, or the same speed with less memory, or fewer hitches – not necessarily a higher average frame rate.
  • “Adjusted” or “updated” describes a change without saying in which direction. Treat it as something to re-test.
  • “Stability improvements” usually means crash fixes, not performance.
  • “Various fixes” tells you nothing; if you had a specific problem, test it again.
  • Numbers such as “up to 20% faster” describe the best case the developer measured, not an average across systems.

The plus, tilde and minus

We mark each relevant line with one of three signs. The sign describes what you need to do, not whether the developer made a good decision. A tilde line can be the most important one in an update, because it changes something you tuned without saying so loudly.

Sorting by platform

Read only what applies to your platform, but read all of it. PC notes often mention specific graphics vendors or APIs (DirectX 12, Vulkan). Console notes mention modes: performance, quality or fidelity, 120 Hz, VRR, or – on PS5 Pro – enhanced modes. Switch 2 notes may treat docked and handheld play separately. Steam Deck and other handheld PCs are often listed under PC, sometimes with their own lines. A line without a platform usually applies to all.

After reading: a short plan

Write down the two or three lines that matter for you, decide what to test, and then test calmly – after shader compilation is done. The 10-minute re-test routine turns this into a checklist, and the Patch Decoder can export your notes and checklist as text so you can keep them with your settings.

Hotfixes, staged rollouts and platform delays

Big updates are rarely the end of the story. A hotfix often follows within days, and its notes are usually shorter and sometimes published only on social media or the developer’s forum. Updates can also arrive on different platforms on different days, because each platform has its own certification process. When something changes for you a few days after a big patch, check for a hotfix before assuming the main update caused it.

Some developers also change things on the server side without a client update – for example balancing values or event settings. Such changes rarely affect graphics, but they can affect performance in busy multiplayer modes when more players gather in one place. If performance changes without any download, that is a likely reason.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I find patch notes for my game?

On the developer’s or publisher’s website, the game’s news page in the platform store or launcher, and often in the game’s main menu. Use the official source rather than summaries.

What does “stability improvements” mean?

Usually crash and freeze fixes. It rarely means higher frame rates.

Why did my settings change after an update?

Updates can reset settings, rename presets, remove options or change defaults. Check your settings menu after every larger update.

Should I trust “up to X% faster” claims?

Treat them as the best case under the developer’s test conditions. Re-test in your own scenes to see what changes for you.
