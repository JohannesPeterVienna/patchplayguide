Shader compilation stutter after an update: what to do

By the Patch Play Guide editorial team, AdStern

Reviewed November 2, 20264 min readHow we check pages

The game ran smoothly yesterday. After an update it hitches every time you turn a corner or an explosion goes off – and then, a few minutes later, it is fine again. That pattern almost always means shader compilation: the game is building the small GPU programs it needs for your specific graphics card and driver, and an update threw away the old ones.

What shaders are, and why they are compiled on your PC

Shaders are the small programs that run on the graphics card to calculate the color of surfaces, light, shadows and effects. A large game uses thousands of combinations of them. With modern graphics interfaces such as DirectX 12 and Vulkan, shaders are combined with GPU state into so-called pipeline state objects, and these have to be compiled into machine code for your exact graphics card and driver version.

Consoles have one fixed hardware configuration, so developers can ship the compiled shaders with the game. PCs come in countless combinations of graphics cards and drivers, so compilation usually happens on your machine – either up front in a “compiling shaders” step, or on the fly the first time a material or effect appears on screen. When it happens on the fly, the frame has to wait for the compiler, and you see a hitch.

Why updates bring the stutter back

Compiled shaders are stored in a cache on your drive so that they can be reused next time. That cache is tied to the game’s shader code and to the graphics driver. When either changes, old entries no longer match:

What usually invalidates the shader cache
EventEffect on the cacheWhat you notice
Game update that changes shaders or materialsAffected shaders must be compiled againStutter in areas and effects you had already seen
Graphics driver updateDriver cache usually rebuiltStutter in every game for the first sessions
Changing graphics settings or upscalerNew shader variants may be neededShort stutter after the change
Moving to a new GPUCache rebuilt completelyStutter everywhere at first
Clearing the cache manuallyEverything compiled againStutter returns until rebuilt

This is why a patch can seem to make a game “run worse” for an evening, and then the problem disappears. Judging performance in the first minutes after an update – or after a driver update – mostly measures the compiler, not the game.

How to recognize shader stutter

  • It happens the first time you see an area, an enemy type or an effect – and much less the second time.
  • It started right after a game or driver update, or after changing settings.
  • The average frame rate is fine; individual frames take much longer (the frame-time graph shows spikes).
  • It gets better over a session, and better again in the next one.

Stutter that returns in the same places on every run is more likely asset streaming, full video memory or a CPU spike – the Bottleneck Finder separates these cases.

What helps

  • Let precompilation finish. If the game shows a “compiling shaders” step or progress bar in the menu, wait for it. Skipping it moves the work into gameplay.
  • Give it time. Play calmly through the first areas after an update before judging performance. Re-test afterwards with the 10-minute routine.
  • Keep the game on an SSD. Cache reads and asset loading are slower on hard drives, which makes hitches worse.
  • On Steam and Steam Deck, leave Shader Pre-Caching enabled in the Steam settings; for supported games, Steam can download precompiled shader data for your configuration.
  • Avoid driver updates right before an important session – install them when you have time for the cache to rebuild.
  • Check the driver’s shader cache size setting if your driver offers one; a cache that is too small can evict entries and compile them again.

What does not help

Lowering graphics settings rarely removes shader stutter – the compiler still has to build the shaders that are needed, and different settings can even require new variants. Clearing the shader cache “to fix stutter” usually makes things worse for a while, because everything has to be compiled again; do it only when a specific guide for a specific problem (for example a corrupted cache after a crash) recommends it.

A faster processor does shorten each compile, because compilation is CPU work, but it does not remove the pattern. The real fix is on the developer side: precompiling all needed shaders during loading. Many updates mention exactly this. The Patch Decoder puts such lines in the “Shaders & loading” category, and a tilde (~) on “shaders are rebuilt” is your cue to expect a stuttery first session.

Consoles and handhelds

On PS5, Xbox and Switch 2, shaders are typically compiled for the fixed hardware and shipped with the game, so post-update shader stutter is much less common than on PC. On the Steam Deck the situation is in between: it is one hardware configuration, which is what makes downloadable precompiled shader data from Steam effective there. Windows handheld PCs behave like any other PC.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my game stutter after an update and then get better?

The update or a new driver invalidated the shader cache. The game compiles shaders again the first time each effect appears; once they are cached, the stutter fades.

Should I delete the shader cache to fix stutter?

Usually not. Deleting it forces everything to be compiled again. Only clear it when a fix for a specific problem, such as a corrupted cache, tells you to.

Do graphics settings affect shader stutter?

Only a little. Lower settings do not remove compilation, and changing settings can even require new shader variants.

Does an SSD fix shader stutter?

It does not stop compilation, but it speeds up cache and asset loading, so hitches are shorter. Modern games generally expect an SSD.
