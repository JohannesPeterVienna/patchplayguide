PS5 performance vs fidelity modes explained

Published by AdStern, Vienna

Reviewed November 24, 20266 min readHow we check pages

Many PS5 games let you choose between a performance mode and a fidelity, quality or resolution mode, and some add a balanced mode. Sony’s own definition is one sentence: performance mode makes motion smoother, resolution mode gives higher graphic quality. What that means in a particular game is the developer’s decision. This guide shows what developers have published about their modes, why some modes need a 120 Hz TV, and how to set a default.

The trade-off, in the developers’ own words

Sony defines the two options in the PS5’s Game Presets: “Make motion in your game smoother with performance mode, or have higher graphic quality in resolution mode. This applies only to games that offer these modes.” The PS5 itself does not set a resolution or frame rate for a mode; the game does. The clearest way to see the trade is in the descriptions developers have published for their own games:

The pattern across these examples: the quality side spends the frame time on resolution and ray tracing, the performance side on frame rate. Two of them show that the line is not always “ray tracing on or off” – Black Flag Resynced keeps ray-traced global illumination in every mode and drops only the reflections, and Saros uses dynamic resolution to hold 60 fps in gameplay, switching to 30 fps only in key story cinematics.

The arithmetic behind the trade is simple. At 30 frames per second each frame may take up to 33.3 milliseconds; at 60 only 16.7. To halve the time per frame, a game has to drop work – pixels, effects or both – which is what the performance side of every row above does.

Why 40 fps balanced modes need 120 Hz

A 60 Hz TV refreshes every 16.7 ms. A 30 fps game shows each frame for exactly two refreshes, a 60 fps game for one – both are even. A 40 fps game would need 1.5 refreshes per frame, which a 60 Hz signal cannot do evenly, so motion would judder. A 120 Hz signal refreshes every 8.3 ms, and 40 fps fits exactly three refreshes per frame (25 ms). That is why 40 fps modes are tied to 120 Hz output.

For the same reason, 40 fps feels like a bigger step up from 30 than the numbers suggest: it saves 8.3 ms per frame, exactly half of the 16.7 ms that 60 fps saves.

Worked example: which frame rates fit which output

The same arithmetic decides for every target, not only for 40 fps. Divide the output’s refresh rate by the frame rate: a whole number means every frame is held equally long; anything else means alternating holds unless VRR is active.

On the PS5, 120 Hz Output and VRR are under Settings → Screen and Video → Video Output; what each switch does, in Sony’s words, and what the TV and cable need is in VRR and 120 Hz on consoles. The default mode lives elsewhere: Settings → Saved Data and Game/App Settings → Game Presets → “Performance Mode or Resolution Mode”, which Sony says applies only to games that offer these modes.

Try it with your values. Check any frame rate against your TV’s refresh rate and VRR window.

How to choose

This part is our suggestion, not a measurement. Performance suits action games and anything where camera movement and reaction time matter, and players who are sensitive to 30 fps motion; on a 4K TV at a normal distance the resolution difference is smaller than the frame-rate difference. Fidelity suits slow, cinematic games on a large screen, especially when ray-traced lighting is part of the look – check the game’s own description, because, as the table shows, the quality side is not always the only one with ray tracing. Balanced (40 fps) is the middle way if your TV supports 120 Hz.

Try each mode for a few minutes in the same area, turning the camera and moving quickly. How it feels in motion is a better guide than a screenshot.

When updates change modes

Updates can add or change modes: a new 40 fps option, a changed resolution range, ray tracing added to a mode, a PS5 Pro patch – and since October 2026 also QSSR, Sony’s AI upscaler for the standard PS5, which games add as an extra graphics option through their own updates. Marvel’s Wolverine and Ghost of Yōtei were the first; Sony named six more on November 13, 2026. The console upgrade reference lists them with the developers’ statements. After such an update, check that your chosen mode is still selected – in the game and in the system Game Presets – and compare again. The Patch Decoder puts mode lines in “Console & handheld modes” so you can filter for PS5.

Frequently asked questions

Is performance mode or fidelity mode better on PS5?

Neither in general. Sony describes performance mode as smoother motion and resolution mode as higher graphic quality; each game decides the details, often 60 fps against 30 fps with a higher resolution or more ray tracing.

Why does my PS5 game not show a 40 fps mode?

A 40 fps mode paces evenly only on a 120 Hz signal. Check that “120 Hz Output” is on and that your TV and HDMI port support 120 Hz. Not every game offers 40 fps.

What does the Game Presets setting do?

It sets “Performance Mode or Resolution Mode” as a default. Sony: it applies only to games that offer these modes; games can still offer their own options.

Does VRR help in a 30 fps mode?

VRR follows the frame rate only inside your display’s VRR window. If the window starts above 30 Hz – look it up in the TV’s manual – a steady 30 fps mode gains little.
