PS5 performance vs fidelity modes explained

By the Patch Play Guide editorial team, AdStern

Reviewed November 2, 20264 min readHow we check pages

Many PS5 games ask you to choose between a performance mode and a fidelity, quality or resolution mode – sometimes with a balanced mode in between. The names differ from game to game, but the trade-off is the same: a higher frame rate or a sharper, richer image. This guide explains what each mode usually changes, which TV features they depend on, and how to choose.

The basic trade-off

A console has a fixed amount of GPU and CPU power. A game can spend it on more pixels and effects per frame, or on more frames per second – not both. A fidelity mode (also called quality or resolution mode) usually targets 30 frames per second with a higher resolution and more effects, sometimes including ray tracing. A performance mode usually targets 60 frames per second with a lower resolution and fewer or simpler effects. Some games target 30 and 60, others 60 and 120.

At 30 frames per second each frame may take up to 33.3 milliseconds; at 60 only 16.7. Halving the time per frame means the game has to cut a lot of GPU work, which is why performance modes often use dynamic resolution – the internal resolution drops in heavy scenes – and an upscaler to reach the display resolution.

Why 40 fps balanced modes need 120 Hz

A 60 Hz TV refreshes every 16.7 ms. A 30 fps game shows each frame for exactly two refreshes, a 60 fps game for one – both are even. A 40 fps game would need 1.5 refreshes per frame, which a 60 Hz signal cannot do evenly, so motion would judder. A 120 Hz signal refreshes every 8.3 ms, and 40 fps fits exactly three refreshes per frame (25 ms). That is why 40 fps modes appear only when the PS5 outputs 120 Hz.

For the same reason, 40 fps feels like a bigger step up from 30 than the numbers suggest: it saves 8.3 ms per frame, exactly half of the 16.7 ms that 60 fps saves.

The PS5 settings that matter

  • Game Presets. In the PS5 settings under Saved Data and Game/App Settings, the Game Presets section lets you set a default preference – Performance Mode or Resolution Mode – that supported games use when they start. Games can still offer their own menu.
  • 120 Hz output. Under Screen and Video → Video Output, “Enable 120 Hz Output” needs to be on (Automatic) for 120 Hz and 40 fps modes. It requires a TV or monitor and HDMI port that support 120 Hz.
  • VRR. In the same menu, VRR can be enabled for compatible HDMI 2.1 displays. It smooths frame-rate dips in performance modes. There is also an option to apply VRR to games without official support, which can help some and cause issues in others.
  • TV game mode. On the TV itself, a game mode or low-latency setting removes processing that adds input delay.

How to choose

Choose performance if you play action games, shooters or anything where camera movement and reaction time matter, if you sit at a normal distance from a 4K TV where resolution differences are small, or if you are sensitive to 30 fps motion. This is the most common choice and a sensible default.

Choose fidelity for slow, cinematic games on a large screen, especially when ray-traced lighting is part of the look, and if 30 fps does not bother you.

Choose balanced (40 fps) if your TV supports 120 Hz and the performance mode looks too soft, or if the fidelity mode feels sluggish.

Try each mode for a few minutes in the same area, turning the camera and moving quickly. How it feels in motion is a better guide than a screenshot.

When updates change modes

Updates often adjust modes: a new 40 fps option, a changed resolution range, ray tracing added to performance mode, or support for the PS5 Pro. After such an update, check that your chosen mode is still selected – both the game menu and the system Game Presets – and compare again if a mode’s target changed. Frequently asked questions

Is performance mode or fidelity mode better on PS5?

Neither is better in general. Performance mode gives a higher frame rate and faster response; fidelity mode gives a sharper image and more effects at a lower frame rate. Most players prefer performance in action games.

Why does my PS5 game not show a 40 fps mode?

Most 40 fps modes need 120 Hz output. Check that “Enable 120 Hz Output” is on and that your TV and HDMI port support 120 Hz. Not every game offers 40 fps.

What does the Game Presets setting do?

It sets a default preference for performance or resolution that supported games use. Games can still offer their own options in their menus.

Does VRR help in fidelity mode?

It helps most when the frame rate fluctuates within the display’s VRR range. Many 30 fps modes sit below the range of typical TVs, where VRR has less effect.
