Xbox Series S vs X: why game modes differ

Published by AdStern, Vienna

Reviewed November 2, 20264 min readHow we check pages

The Xbox Series S and Series X play the same games, but often not with the same options. A game may offer a 60 fps mode and ray tracing on Series X and only one mode on Series S. The reason is not arbitrary: the two consoles share nearly the same CPU but differ greatly in GPU power and memory.

What differs between the consoles

Xbox Series X and Series S (Microsoft’s published specifications, rounded)
Series XSeries S
CPU8 cores, Zen 2, up to 3.8 GHz8 cores, Zen 2, up to 3.6 GHz
GPU computeAbout 12 teraflopsAbout 4 teraflops
Memory16 GB GDDR610 GB GDDR6
Resolution target (Microsoft)4K1440p
Disc driveYes (digital model also available)No

The teraflop figure is only a rough indicator of GPU power; architecture is the same, so it is a fair comparison between these two consoles.

The key points: the CPUs are close, so game logic, physics and simulation run at similar speed. The GPU of the Series S has roughly a third of the compute of the Series X, and the Series S has less memory – and a smaller share of it is available to games. Developers have to fit the same game into both.

Why modes are cut or changed

Lower resolution first. The Series S was designed for a 1440p target instead of 4K, so most games run at a lower render resolution there, often with dynamic resolution and upscaling. On a 1080p or 1440p TV that is perfectly fine; on a large 4K TV the image is noticeably softer.

Memory is the hard limit. Less memory means smaller texture budgets, fewer high-detail assets and less room for ray-tracing data. Unlike resolution, memory cannot be scaled smoothly, which is why some features – ray tracing, very high texture settings, sometimes even a 60 fps mode with all its buffers – are dropped on Series S.

CPU-heavy games stay similar. Because the CPUs are close, a game that is CPU-limited at 30 fps on Series X will usually also be 30 fps on Series S. A 60 fps mode on Series X that relies on GPU headroom is the one most likely to disappear on Series S.

Xbox settings worth checking

  • Refresh rate. Settings → General → TV & display options: set 120 Hz if your TV supports it. Some games offer 120 fps or 40 fps modes only with 120 Hz output.
  • Variable refresh rate. Under Video modes in the same menu, allow VRR for a compatible display. It smooths frame-rate dips.
  • Auto Low Latency Mode (ALLM). Lets the TV switch to its game mode automatically.
  • Game-specific modes. Many games show performance and quality options only on Series X, or name them differently on Series S.

Setting names can change with system updates; the VRR and 120 Hz guide explains the TV side as well.

Choosing a mode on each console

On Series X the choice works like on PS5: performance for action and responsiveness, quality for cinematic games – see performance vs fidelity modes, which applies equally here. On Series S, if a game offers both modes, the performance mode often drops resolution considerably; on a 1080p TV that is usually a good trade, on a large 4K TV judge the softness yourself.

If a game offers only one mode on Series S, it is often the developer’s decision to keep memory and frame-rate stable. Patches sometimes add modes to Series S later, after optimization; the Patch Decoder helps you spot such lines with the platform filter set to Xbox.

Patches and the Series S

Series S versions are often optimized after launch: memory savings allow better textures, new modes or a more stable frame rate. Equally, a large content update can make a game heavier. When an update mentions “Series S” or “Xbox Series X|S”, re-check your mode and compare in a familiar scene, following the re-test routine.

Which TV suits which console

The Series S makes most sense with a 1080p or 1440p display, or a 4K TV viewed from a distance. The Series X is aimed at 4K TVs, and its 60 and 120 fps modes benefit most from an HDMI 2.1 TV with VRR. On either console, the TV’s game mode affects input delay more than most in-game options.

One purchase, the right version

Microsoft’s Smart Delivery system, used by many games, installs the version that fits your console automatically, so the same purchase gets Series S or Series X optimizations as appropriate. The practical consequence: when you move from a Series S to a Series X, or the other way round, a game’s available modes can change after the new version is installed. Check the mode settings in each game after switching consoles, just as after an update.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my game have no performance mode on Xbox Series S?

Usually because the Series S has much less GPU power and memory. Developers sometimes drop a mode to keep resolution and memory use acceptable.

Is the Xbox Series S CPU slower than the Series X?

Only slightly – both use eight Zen 2 cores, with slightly lower clock speeds on Series S. The big differences are GPU power and memory.

Does Series S support 120 Hz?

Yes, Microsoft lists up to 120 fps for both consoles, in games that offer it and with a compatible display.

Why is the Series S image softer on my 4K TV?

Series S games usually render at a lower resolution, targeting 1440p or below, and upscale to 4K. The difference is most visible on large 4K screens.
