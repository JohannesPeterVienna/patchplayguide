Xbox Series S vs X: why game modes differ

Published by AdStern, Vienna

Reviewed November 24, 20264 min readHow we check pages

The Xbox Series S and Series X play the same games, but not always with the same options: a game may offer a 60 fps mode and ray tracing on Series X and fewer choices on Series S. Microsoft’s own specifications explain why. They list the same CPU for both consoles, but a GPU with a third of the compute, less and slower memory, and a lower resolution target for the Series S.

What differs between the consoles

Xbox Series X and Series S as listed on Microsoft’s product pages (read November 24, 2026)

CPU8 cores at 3.8 GHz (3.6 GHz with SMT), custom Zen 28 cores at 3.8 GHz (3.6 GHz with SMT), custom Zen 2
GPU12 TFLOPS, 52 compute units at 1.825 GHz, custom RDNA 24 TFLOPS, 20 compute units at 1.565 GHz, custom RDNA 2
Memory16 GB GDDR6, 320-bit bus10 GB GDDR6, 128-bit bus
Memory bandwidth10 GB at 560 GB/s, 6 GB at 336 GB/s8 GB at 224 GB/s, 2 GB at 56 GB/s
Gaming resolutionTrue 4K1440p
Performance targetUp to 120 fpsUp to 120 fps
HDMI featuresALLM, HDMI VRR, AMD FreeSyncALLM, HDMI VRR, AMD FreeSync

Microsoft’s wording, shortened. The CPU line is identical on both pages; the differences are GPU, memory and the resolution target.

Because the processors are listed identically, work that runs on the CPU – game logic, physics, simulation – has the same budget on both consoles. Everything that depends on the GPU and on memory has less on the Series S: a third of the compute, 10 instead of 16 GB, and a fast memory pool that is both smaller (8 against 10 GB) and less than half as fast (224 against 560 GB/s). Developers have to fit the same game into both.

Worked example: the gap in numbers

Microsoft’s figures allow a simple comparison of what each console is asked to do at its resolution target – 4K for the Series X, 1440p for the Series S.

Targets and resources side by side (Microsoft’s figures; ratios calculated)
Series XSeries SRatio X : S
Pixels at the resolution target3840×2160 = 8.29 MP2560×1440 = 3.69 MP2.25 : 1
GPU compute12 TFLOPS4 TFLOPS3 : 1
Memory16 GB10 GB1.6 : 1
Bandwidth of the fast memory pool560 GB/s224 GB/s2.5 : 1

Two things follow from the arithmetic. The GPU gap (3 to 1) is larger than the pixel gap between the two targets (2.25 to 1): a Series X mode that uses its GPU fully at 4K cannot run with the same settings at 1440p on the Series S – it has to give up about a quarter more, through a lower internal resolution or cheaper effects. And memory shrinks least in the ratio, but textures and world data do not get smaller when the resolution does. Which of these a developer chooses is different in every game; the game’s store page and patch notes say what its Series S version offers.

What the figures mean for modes

A frame-rate mode that relies on spare GPU time on the Series X is the one with the least room on the Series S, because the CPU budget is the same but the GPU budget is a third. A resolution difference is built into the specifications: Microsoft lists 1440p as the gaming resolution of the Series S, so on a large 4K TV its image is upscaled further. Memory-heavy features – high-resolution textures, ray-tracing data – compete for 10 instead of 16 GB. None of this says what a specific game does; it explains why the options can differ at all.

On either console, the choice between modes works as described in PS5 performance vs fidelity modes: quality settings against frame rate, with 40 and 120 fps modes depending on 120 Hz output.

120 Hz on Series X and Series S

Both consoles list a performance target of up to 120 fps, but not at every resolution. Xbox Support: 4K at 120 Hz needs an Xbox Series X, an Ultra High Speed HDMI cable that supports HDMI 2.1 and a TV that supports HDMI 2.1. On a Series S, or with a TV without HDMI 2.1, 120 Hz is still available at a lower resolution – set Refresh rate to 120 Hz under Profile & system → Settings → General → TV & display options, and the console adjusts the resolution if it was set to 4K. That matters for game modes: a 120 fps or 40 fps mode needs the 120 Hz output first.

The other display settings – Allow variable refresh rate, Allow auto low-latency mode, 4K TV details – and the TV side are described in VRR and 120 Hz on consoles.

Patches and the Series S

Updates can change what a Series S version offers in either direction – a new mode after optimization, or a heavier game after a content update. When patch notes mention “Series S” or “Xbox Series X|S”, check your mode and compare in a familiar scene, following the re-test routine. The Patch Decoder filters such lines by platform.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my game have fewer modes on Xbox Series S?

Microsoft lists a GPU with 4 instead of 12 TFLOPS, 10 instead of 16 GB of memory and a 1440p resolution target for the Series S. A developer may drop or change a mode to fit those limits; the game’s store page or patch notes say what it offers.

Is the Xbox Series S CPU slower than the Series X?

Microsoft’s product pages list the same CPU line for both: 8 cores at 3.8 GHz (3.6 GHz with SMT), custom Zen 2. The differences are GPU, memory and the resolution target.

Does Series S support 120 Hz?

Yes. Microsoft lists a performance target of up to 120 fps for both consoles, in games that offer it and with a compatible display. 4K at 120 Hz is a Series X feature.

Why is the Series S image softer on my 4K TV?

Microsoft lists 1440p as the Series S gaming resolution, so a 4K TV has to show an image with fewer pixels than it has. The difference is easiest to see on large screens.
