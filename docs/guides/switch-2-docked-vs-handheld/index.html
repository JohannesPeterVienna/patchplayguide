Switch 2 docked vs handheld: what games change

Published by AdStern, Vienna

Reviewed November 24, 20266 min readHow we check pages

The Nintendo Switch 2 is a handheld with its own 1080p screen and a TV console in the dock. The two have different limits – and Nintendo’s own games show how developers use them, for example 4K on the TV against 1080p on the built-in screen. This guide sets out the limits from Nintendo’s tech specs, what Nintendo states for specific games, what changed with the system updates of 2026, and how to judge it yourself.

Two screens, two sets of limits

In handheld and tabletop mode the game draws for the built-in 7.9-inch screen with 1920×1080 pixels; in TV mode it draws for whatever the TV accepts, up to 4K. Nintendo’s tech specs set the limits for each:

Nintendo’s wording, shortened. 4K, HDR and VRR in TV mode are available in supported games and need a compatible display.

How much a game changes between the two is decided by its developer. For original Switch games, Nintendo states the direction itself: its Handheld Mode Boost setting lets them run undocked “with performance equivalent to TV mode”, which increases power consumption – so the TV mode of those games has more performance to work with.

VRR on the TV since September 2026. System update 23.0.0 added VRR in TV mode and VRR settings under Display in System Settings: “When both the software and TV support it, video will be output at a refresh rate that matches the game’s processing.” The dock needs a firmware update first (System Settings → System → Dock Update). Nintendo’s update history (Americas) dates 23.0.0 to September 9, 2026; its features page says September 10, 2026. On the built-in screen, VRR was available from launch.

Worked example: pixels per second in each mode

Multiply resolution by frame rate and you see what each output combination demands at most.

These are output limits, not what a game renders internally.

The table explains two things. A 4K TV shows four times the pixels of the built-in screen, so a game has to render more, upscale or drop detail to fill it. And 1440p at 120 fps is almost as demanding as 4K at 60 fps (442 against 498 million pixels per second), which is why a 120 fps mode comes with a lower resolution – exactly what Nintendo states for Metroid Prime 4 below.

The controls are in System Settings. Nintendo’s settings overview lists TV resolution with Automatic, 720p, 1080p, 1440p and 2160p (4K), and an on/off switch for 120Hz output; the VRR settings came with update 23.0.0.

What Nintendo states for specific games

Nintendo describes the docked and handheld output of several Switch 2 Editions in numbers. Two show the pattern clearly:

Both display modes of Metroid Prime 4 support HDR. Other Switch 2 Editions and free updates are listed with Nintendo’s wording in the console upgrade reference.

The direction is the same in both games: the TV gets the higher resolution, the built-in screen a lower one, and a 120 fps mode costs resolution on both. What a game does with the extra performance in TV mode – effects, view distance – is not part of these descriptions.

Switch 1 games on Switch 2

Original Switch games can run on Switch 2 in three ways. A Nintendo Switch 2 Edition is the original game with a paid upgrade pack; Nintendo lists improvements such as higher frame rates and resolution, HDR, faster loading and sometimes new content. Free updates improve selected games on Switch 2 – most often the image quality on the Switch 2 screen and on high-resolution TVs, in some games the frame rate or HDR. All other compatible games run unchanged – unless you turn on Handheld Mode Boost.

Handheld Mode Boost arrived with system update 22.0.0 (March 16, 2026) under System Settings → System → Nintendo Switch Software Handling and is off by default; since 23.0.0 it can also be switched from the Quick Settings. Nintendo’s description: compatible Switch games run in handheld and tabletop mode as if in TV mode, which “can result in improved visuals” but may increase power consumption. Some games are unaffected, it has no effect on Switch 2 games, it may stop touch-screen controls from working, and attached Joy-Con 2 controllers are treated as a Pro Controller. The console upgrade reference lists Nintendo’s statements per game.

How to compare yourself

Play the same short section docked and handheld: a busy area with camera movement and some distant detail. Watch for three things: smoothness while turning the camera, sharpness of distant objects and text, and whether effects such as shadows or reflections look different. If a game offers its own mode choice, as Metroid Prime 4 does, compare both modes in both places – the right choice on the TV is not necessarily the right one on the built-in screen, which shows at most 1080p anyway.

Battery life is the other half of the handheld decision. Nintendo gives about 2 to 6.5 hours and says it depends on the games you play; Handheld Mode Boost moves a Switch game towards the short end, because it raises power consumption. Patches can change one mode and leave the other alone – read the notes for the words “TV mode” and “handheld mode”, and use the re-test routine after an update.

Frequently asked questions

Do Switch 2 games run at a lower resolution in handheld mode?

The built-in screen shows at most 1920×1080, while TV mode goes up to 4K. Nintendo’s own examples follow that: Metroid Prime 4 runs at 4K or 1080p on the TV and at 1080p or 720p in handheld mode.

Does the Switch 2 support 120 Hz?

Yes. Nintendo lists VRR up to 120 Hz on the built-in screen, and 120 fps TV output when 1920×1080 or 2560×1440 is selected. Whether a game uses it is up to the developer.

Does the Switch 2 support VRR on the TV?

Yes, since system update 23.0.0 in September 2026. It needs a dock firmware update, a TV that supports VRR and a game that supports it; the settings are under Display in System Settings.

What is Handheld Mode Boost?

A system setting since update 22.0.0 that makes compatible original Switch games run undocked as if in TV mode. Nintendo says it can improve visuals but raises power consumption; it has no effect on Switch 2 games.
