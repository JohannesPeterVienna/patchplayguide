About & method

About Patch Play Guide

Patch Play Guide explains what game updates and graphics settings change on your own screen – on PC, PS5, Xbox, Switch 2 and handheld PCs – and how to check it yourself. It is built for players who want to understand their settings rather than copy a list.

Who publishes the site

The site is published by AdStern, an advertising agency based in Vienna, Austria; the full details are in the imprint. Patch Play Guide is financed by advertising on the guide, tool and overview pages; the imprint, privacy policy and contact page carry no advertising. No game publisher, platform holder or hardware manufacturer pays for coverage, and the guides contain no affiliate links. We are not affiliated with any company whose products we mention.

How the guides are written

Every guide is written for this site, with the help of AI tools, around one question a player actually has – “why does my game stutter after an update?” rather than a product announcement. Each guide explains the mechanism behind the problem, gives a concrete order of actions, and states which platforms it applies to. Technical statements are based on vendor documentation, platform documentation and long-established, widely documented graphics behavior; each page lists its sources at the end.

We deliberately describe the cost of settings in qualitative terms – minimal, low, moderate, high – and call them “typical”. Performance depends on the game, the scene, the hardware and the driver, and percentages from one test system are often copied far beyond the conditions they were measured under. We do not publish benchmark figures we have not measured ourselves. If we publish our own measurements in the future, each will state the exact hardware, software versions, scene and method.

How we handle patch notes

Patch notes belong to their publishers. We never copy them. The Patch Decoder works with a clearly fictional example game and with lines you paste yourself from the official source; the sorting happens in your browser. We currently do not publish summaries of specific real-world patches, because each summary would have to be verified line by line against the official notes and kept up to date with hotfixes. Our guides instead teach you to read any patch note and to re-test after an update.

How the tools work

  • Patch Decoder sorts lines with a published set of word rules, in a fixed order, into a change type (+, ~, −), a category and platforms. The rules and their limits are described on the tool page.
  • Settings Translator rates twenty common options with qualitative GPU, CPU and VRAM levels and a “visible loss” rating, and ranks them with a simple published formula for your bottleneck.
  • Bottleneck Finder is a decision tree of ten questions and seventeen results; each result links to the guide that explains it.

The logic of all three tools is covered by automated tests: for example, that every answer in the finder leads somewhere, that every result is reachable, and that the decoder sorts reference lines correctly.

How pages are checked

Guides are researched from vendor and platform documentation as known at the time of writing and checked in a separate review pass against those sources before publishing. That review consists of independent AI review passes and automated tests; there is no team of human editors behind the site. Calculations such as frame times and upscaler resolutions are recalculated during that review, and the automated tests check the tools, the internal links and the page structure. AdStern, the publisher, decides what is published and is responsible for it. Platform menu paths and vendor features can change between system and driver updates, so a path on our pages may no longer match your console or PC – please report any difference via the contact page. Statements we could not verify with certainty are phrased cautiously or left out. Each page shows the date of its last review and has a change log; when we correct something, the date and the log change.

What the site cannot do

We cannot see your system. A guide can tell you what usually helps; only a test on your hardware tells you what helps you. Platform menus and vendor features change with system and driver updates – if a menu path on our pages no longer matches, the platform’s support site has the current one, and we appreciate a note. We do not give advice on hardware purchases, modding, cheating or circumventing platform rules.

No logos, key art or screenshots

All diagrams on this site are our own drawings. We show no game logos, characters, key art or screenshots, and mention game, platform and technology names only as text to describe compatibility and settings.

Your data

Everything you enter in the tools, including “My setup”, stays in your browser’s local storage. There is no account. Advertising and analytics are described in the privacy policy.

History

Patch Play Guide started as a single page with local tools for organizing patch notes and comparing frame rates. In November 2026 it was rebuilt from scratch with the in-depth guides and the three tools you see now.
